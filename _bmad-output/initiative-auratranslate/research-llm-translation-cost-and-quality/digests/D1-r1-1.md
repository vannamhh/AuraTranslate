# D1 r1-1: context packaging for LLM translation (accessed 2026-10-27)
Caveat: only DelTA full text was read (WebFetch). Everything else is from search-result summaries, not opened papers, so confidence is capped at medium.

## Findings
- **[LB]** DelTA (ICLR 2025), setup: GPT-3.5-Turbo-0125, GPT-4o-mini, Qwen2-7B and Qwen2-72B; IWSLT2017 En<->Zh/De/Fr/Ja plus Guofeng Zh->En. Averaged over the four backbones, En->Xx sCOMET was: sentence 81.22, "Context" (3 previous source-target pairs) 82.95, DelTA 84.36. Xx->En: 81.53 / 84.07 / 84.69. LTCR-1 consistency, En->Xx: 77.27 / 79.02 / 81.63. Previous pairs capture most of the sCOMET gain; the memory agent adds about 1.4 sCOMET and 2.6 LTCR-1 on top. · https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08143v2 · arXiv/ICLR · 2024-10 (v2 later) · medium-high · quality-number
- **[LB]** Same paper, Guofeng Zh->En (web-fiction-like): Sentence -> DelTA LTCR-1 was 61.58->85.57 (GPT-3.5), 58.82->88.94 (4o-mini), 37.00->85.50 (Qwen2-7B), 58.00->86.93 (Qwen2-72B). sCOMET rose only about 1 point, or 3.3 for Qwen2-7B. The big gain is in name/term consistency, not COMET. · same URL · 2024-10 · medium-high · quality-number
- **[LB]** Window-size effect (DelTA Table 1): translating a window of 30 sentences per call dropped 8 sentences, and a window of 50 dropped 10. Larger windows improve consistency but cause omissions. DelTA translates one sentence at a time to avoid omission and keeps alignment. · same URL · 2024-10 · medium · failure-mode
- DelTA ablation (GPT-3.5, En->Zh): short-term memory alone raised sCOMET to 84.50 but lowered LTCR-1 to 77.89 (from 80.27 sentence-level). The Proper Noun Records component adds about 3.95 LTCR-1. Full: sCOMET 84.70, LTCR-1 86.44. Take-away: recent-context alone does not give term consistency; an explicit name/term record does. · same URL · medium · quality-number
- DelTA reports NO token, call or dollar cost. Its limitations section admits frequent LLM calls and longer runtime. Doc2Doc ran out of GPU memory at 490 sentences. The cost multiple is unmeasured in the paper. · same URL · medium · pricing gap
- Doc2Doc (10 sentences per turn in one chat) matched or beat Sentence on LTCR-1 (84.18 vs 80.51 Xx->En) but its output was not sentence-aligned, so sCOMET could not be computed. · same URL · medium · failure-mode (misalignment)
- Karpinska and Iyyer (WMT23): translating a whole literary paragraph beats sentence-by-sentence in human preference, but critical errors persist, including omissions. The paragraph variant had more omissions than the sentence variant, plus some repetition. History source, 2023. · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03245 · WMT · 2023 · medium (search summary only) · failure-mode
- 2025 follow-up on WMT24 documents: multi-turn "source-primed" conversation beats single-turn and segment-level translation in the literary, education and news domains. It includes an omission-in-long-documents analysis; numbers not retrieved. · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.10494 · arXiv · 2025-03 · low-medium · method
- arXiv 2410.20941: prompting an instruction-tuned LLM with a whole document improved over sentence-by-sentence. BLEU often prefers sentence outputs, so BLEU is misleading for document-level evaluation. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20941 · arXiv · 2024-10 · medium · quality-number
- SEGALE (EMNLP 2025): extends metrics to long documents via sentence re-alignment. Reports that many open-weight LLMs translate poorly at their advertised maximum context length. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17249 · arXiv/EMNLP · 2025-09 · medium · failure-mode
- MT Summit 2025 (Mohammed and Niculae): across 9 LLMs, LLM gains from context were weak for pronoun translation; the paper suggests context-aware fine-tuning. · https://aclanthology.org/2025.mtsummit-1.10 · ACL Anthology · 2025 · low-medium · quality-number
- Terminology, WMT25 shared task: DuTerm (En->De/Es/Ru) found flexible, LLM-judged glossary use beat strict constraint enforcement. MULTITAN (En->Es IT domain) combined in-context glossary and placeholders; term consistency above 81%, precision modest. Neither tested filtering the glossary to entries present in the passage vs the whole glossary; no cost comparison found. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07461 and https://aclanthology.org/2025.wmt-1.108 (as given in search) · WMT/arXiv · 2025 · low-medium · method
- TM retrieval: fuzzy-match few-shot beats random examples; returns diminish after a handful (about 2 -> 10 shots gives smaller increments). A 2026 EAMT paper (EMEA medical, En-Ro/En-De) found token-based fuzzy matching beat embedding retrieval in automatic scores, while an earlier paper favoured embeddings, so the results conflict. A 358-sentence TM still helped. · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.13294 ; https://aclanthology.org/2026.eamt-1.34.pdf ; https://aclanthology.org/2026.eamt-1.3.pdf · arXiv/EAMT · 2023 and 2026 · medium-low (summary only; domain is technical, not fiction) · quality-number
- TransAgents (EMNLP 2024 demo; preprint 2405.11804, GPT-4 Turbo): the authors claim about 80x cheaper than human translation and human preference over references on literary text, but lower d-BLEU; weaker on contemporary genres. Zh->En on WMT23 literary set. No LLM-API cost multiple vs a single call was found. Authors' own numbers. · https://arxiv.org/html/2405.11804v1 ; https://tech.slator.com/tencent-ai-introduces-llm-based-virtual-lsp-for-literary-translation/ · 2024 · low-medium · pricing/quality
- Vi-specific: no benchmark of pronoun/kinship-term accuracy for Zh->Vi or En->Vi fiction found. A practitioner on GitHub (TranslateBooksWithLLMs discussion 238) has Gemini build a relationship/address-form glossary from the full text and attaches it to later prompts. Anecdote, not evaluated. · https://github.com/hydropix/TranslateBooksWithLLMs/discussions/238 · GitHub · undated · low · method

## Per question
1. Q1: Evidence says document or paragraph context beats isolated sentences in human and LLM-judge evals (BLEU misleads). Big multi-sentence requests risk omission (DelTA: 8 of 30-window sentences, 10 of 50-window sentences dropped) and misalignment. Sentence-by-sentence with context avoids both, at higher call count.
2. Q2: Three previous source-target pairs gave most of the sCOMET gain in DelTA (about +1.7 En->Xx, +2.5 Xx->En over sentence). Diminishing returns beyond that were not isolated by me; recent context alone did not fix term consistency.
3. Q3: Explicit proper-noun/term records drive consistency (DelTA +3.95 LTCR-1 from them; Guofeng LTCR-1 +25 to +48 points). Passage-filtered vs whole-glossary: NOT found as a measured comparison. WMT25 favours flexible in-context use over hard constraints.
4. Q4: Fuzzy-match few-shot helps, diminishing after a few matches. Evidence is technical-domain and conflicting on retrieval type; none on fiction.
5. Q5: DelTA (+3 COMET, +4-5 consistency, large on names) and TransAgents (human-preferred, 80x cheaper than humans) exist; neither gives an API cost multiple vs baseline. DelTA admits many calls.
6. Q6: Nothing evaluated. Only practitioner workaround (LLM-built relationship/address glossary).
